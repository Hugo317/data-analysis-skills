Cross-cutting pattern

Central Park,NY and Market City,NY keep showing up as the two locations driving demographic splits. Central Park skews young (mean age 35.7) and heavily Single (81.2%); Market City skews older (mean age 48.7) and heavily Married (72.7%) — a pattern confirmed independently by two separate insights (04 and 05).

That same young/Single vs. older/Married split is a plausible shared confounder behind the sharp rating jumps found for heavy smokers (insight 01) and for the 35–44 age dip (insight 02) — both of those insights flagged their own findings as likely confounded rather than proven causal, for exactly this reason.

01

Does smoking affect the way people enjoy food?

Yes, but not gradually. Never and Socially smokers rate food about the same (2.83 vs 2.68); Often smokers rate it dramatically higher (4.10) — a large, sharp jump concentrated entirely in the heaviest-smoking group, not a smooth trend. Service Rating shows a milder version of the same shape. Flagged as more consistent with a confound (see callout above) than a direct effect.

02

What kinds of age enjoy food and service more?

Age matters, but non-monotonically — and food and service peak in different brackets. Raw age-rating correlation is ~0 (a naive check misses this entirely). Once bracketed: under-25s enjoy food most (3.72); 45–54 gets the best service (3.92); the 35–44 bracket is the low point on both (2.46 food, 2.79 service) — possibly explained by that bracket being 57% Married.

Age vs Food Rating r=−0.04, Age vs Service Rating r=−0.04 (both essentially flat) raw corr n.s. — bracketed: Food ANOVA p=0.001 significant, Service ANOVA p=0.023 significant

03

What types of cuisines are most popular by location and by gender?

By location, no cuisine dominates anywhere — leads are narrow (22–36% of a location's diners). China Town,NY leans most toward Seafood (36.4%); Market City,NY is a genuine tie between Indian and Japanese. By gender, Female diners lean most toward French (19.5%); Male diners have no single leader — Filipino, Indian, and Japanese are exactly tied (18.6% each). Italian is the least popular cuisine overall, especially with Male diners (5.1%).

04

Does YOB relate to budget and to area?

Budget — yes, but non-linearly. Raw age-budget correlation is ~0, yet the age-bracket effect is significant: 25–34 year-olds budget noticeably lower (3.36/5) than every other bracket (3.6–4.3) — a dip, not a trend, the same shape as the age/enjoyment insight above. Area — a mild, unconfirmed lead: Central Park,NY skews youngest (mean age 35.7), Market City,NY skews oldest (48.7), but the cross-location difference doesn't clear significance (p≈0.07). Also surfaced: Area code is not 1:1 with Location as first assumed — it's a much finer-grained code (up to 22 per location) — flagged as its own follow-up.

05

Check marital status per area

Marital status clusters by location rather than spreading evenly. Central Park,NY skews heavily Single (81.2% vs. 50.0% dataset-wide); Market City,NY skews heavily Married (72.7% vs. 43.0% overall) — the two best-supported patterns. Independently corroborates insight 04's finding that the same two locations skew youngest/oldest by age.

Chi-square (Location × Marital Status): χ²=32.21, p=0.0037 significant — but 42% of cells have expected count <5, so treat as directional, carried mainly by the two standout locations.
